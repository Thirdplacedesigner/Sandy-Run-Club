Your Morning Journey Starts Here.

The Challenge We Face

The primary barriers identified include a lack of personal time, low motivation, and social or body image concerns, including self-consciousness.

The primary social factor is how active the community one belongs to is. Teens are heavily influenced by how others act, feel or think. We, as the creator of a school-based community, can change these comprehensive attitudes toward physical activity inside the communities and spark an adventurous mindset.

The Sandy Run Initiative

Our initiative is making Sandy Run Club, held from 7:30am to 8:40am on every Friday. The activities in this club are running from the school to the Sandringham beach, dipping in the water, and going to the café to relax, socialise, and get energy from a latte and friends.

By creating this energetic community, I can make the environment where teens have a positive mindset toward working out. Since negative self-consciousness has a tremendous influence to prevent one from physical activity, the energetic atmosphere is pivotal in shaping positive exercising habits.

This run club aims for running for 30 minutes, which meets half the time of Moderate to Vigorous Physical Activity written in the National Physical Activity Guidelines. While it does not fully satisfy the criteria of a 1-hour activity, completing a 30-minute activity before school is highly effective and heightens the chance of conquering the goal.

Overcoming The First Hurdle

Although this run club does not have a minimal number of participants as shown in soccer or basketball clubs, gathering a decent number of students is required to make the atmosphere welcoming, warm, and energetic. It is going to be the biggest barrier for our initiative in the first phase.

Moreover, getting up early for a run is hard for common teenagers who want to use social media, make breakfast, or sleep in. Making the run club an appealing morning adventure for those individuals is a challenging but exciting mission.

Rewarding The Journey

Participants of Sandy Run Club will get tickets for the canteen. They can present them at recess or lunchtime and get free snacks or food. By using a rewarding system, we can encourage people who don’t have a habit of working out in the morning to start a healthy, active habit.

The cost of giving tickets to participants will be supported by fundraising events regularly held inside the college.

Spreading The Word (Mass Media)

Advantage: The primary advantage is that pictures or videos posted on the Sandy Run Club Instagram account can nudge many students to join this club. By gathering attention from many Instagram users besides students, this initiative works beyond Sandringham College and may encourage a huge number of teenagers worldwide to hit the pavement with friends.

Disadvantage: The primary disadvantage is that the posts might stimulate self-consciousness and lead to a negative mindset arising from comparison with highly active people. Moreover, a lack of details in a short video might provide misleading advice for teenagers and cause injuries.

A Settings-Based Approach

A settings-based approach refers to a health promotion strategy that particularly applies to the characteristics of certain places, especially workplaces, schools, or residential buildings.

This method can be effectively applied to our initiative because building communities where people are active and support others’ workouts plays a crucial role in creating a strong workout culture. Moreover, once this community is built, others in the school setting can easily join the journey and have fun with their peers.
